# Week 11 — Space, Tone & Dynamics

> **Phase 3 · Freedom & your voice.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan.

**This week's one goal:** improvise using **half the notes** — and spend what you save on **space, tone, and touch**: rests, bends, slides, vibrato, dynamics, and a warm thumb/finger attack.

**Why this:** here's the open secret about Benson ballads, Peter White, and Chuck Loeb — a huge part of why they sound "smooth" isn't the notes, it's everything *around* them. Space lets a phrase land. Tone and vibrato make one note say more than a run. This is roughly 80% of the "pro" sound, and almost none of it is about playing more.

---

## Less is more — let it breathe

A phrase needs silence around it the way a sentence needs a pause. Beginners fill every beat; pros leave room, and the room is where the music lives. Play a short idea, then **stop** — and let the track answer before you speak again.

Then there's **tone and articulation**. A note bent into pitch, slid into, or given a slow vibrato carries feeling a cleanly-picked note doesn't. Attack matters too — thumb or fingers, soft, on the neck pickup, gives that round smooth-jazz voice. The same notes with a hard bridge-pickup attack are a different, colder song.

## Fretsy's role here is small — and that's fine

This is a feel-and-recording week, over backing tracks. The one genuinely useful Fretsy piece is the **Tone chain (Pro)** — dial in a warm, round, neck-pickup-ish sound with a soft attack and a touch of reverb, so your ear is chasing the right target while you practice. Beyond that, put the app down: this is hands, ears, and a recorder.

## How to practice it

- **The half-notes rule** — improvise a chorus using no more than half the notes you normally would. Force the rests.
- **One-note solo** — improvise on a *single* note; all expression comes from rhythm, dynamics, bends, and vibrato. It reveals how much lives in touch alone.
- **Vibrato & bends** — isolate them. Slow, even vibrato on held chord tones (3rds, 7ths). Bend *into* a target tone rather than away from it.
- **Dynamics** — play a phrase soft, then repeat it loud. Make volume a deliberate choice.

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Tone check — 3–5 min · **Fretsy Tone chain or your amp**
- Dial the warm, round sound: neck pickup, soft attack, a little reverb. Get the target in your ears before you play.

### ② Technique isolation — 10 min
- The day's expressive element (vibrato / bends / dynamics), slow, on **held chord tones** — that's where it's most exposed and most beautiful.

### ③ Sparse improv — 10–15 min · **over a track**
- The half-notes rule. Count the rests as music, not gaps to fill.

### ④ Record & listen — 5 min
- Does it breathe? Mark the moments you rushed to fill silence — those are next session's targets.

---

## Approach

Fight the urge to fill space — that urge is the exact habit we're breaking. When in doubt, leave it out, and let the next note matter more. Tempos slow to moderate; feel over flash, always.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Silence feels awkward."** It always does — to the *player*, never to the listener. Record and play it back; from the outside the space sounds intentional and confident.
- **"My vibrato sounds nervous / uneven."** Slow it way down; make it a deliberate, wide, even wave on a held note. Fast twitchy vibrato is a tension tell.
- **"Half the notes feels boring to play."** Boring to play often sounds *great* to hear. Trust the recording, not the in-the-moment feeling.

## End-of-week checkpoint ✅

- [ ] Record a solo that clearly **breathes** — real rests, not wall-to-wall notes.
- [ ] Expressive, controlled **vibrato** and at least one **bend into** a chord tone.
- [ ] A **dynamic contrast** you placed on purpose (a soft phrase, a louder answer).
- [ ] A **warm tone** you're genuinely happy with.

Not there yet? Do the one-note solo again — it strips everything down to the feel you're building.
