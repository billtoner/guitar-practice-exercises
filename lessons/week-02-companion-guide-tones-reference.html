Guide-Tone Reference

The 3rd & 7th of every quality, in every practice key — and how to grab them as a pair.

Phase 1 · Foundations of automaticity. A reference sheet for Week 2 — Guide Tones (3rds & 7ths). Part of the The Recall Gap 12-week plan.

What this sheet answers: for m7, dom7, and maj7 in every key you drill, what are the 3rd and 7th — and how do you grab them as a pair instead of two separate hunts? This is the lookup table behind the under-a-second recall Week 2 is chasing. Use it to check yourself, never to replace the reflex.

2. Grab them as a pair — the interval between 3rd and 7th

Don't hunt the 3rd, then separately hunt the 7th. Find one and the other sits a fixed distance away — and that distance tells you the quality:

Quality3rd → 7thSound
m7a perfect 5th apartstable, open
maj7a perfect 5th apartstable, open
dom7a tritone aparttense — wants to resolve

That tritone is the whole reason a dom7 sounds unstable and pulls to the next chord (Week 6). m7 and maj7 sit a calm perfect 5th apart. So the width of the pair is itself a quality cue: narrow-and-tense = dominant; open-and-stable = minor or major.

3. The one-note ladder — m7 → dom7 → maj7

The three qualities are a single half-step staircase. Start from m7 and raise one guide tone at a time:

Cm7   →   C7    →   Cmaj7
(E♭,B♭)   (E,B♭)     (E,B)
   └ raise 3rd ┘ └ raise 7th ┘
   ♭3 → 3        ♭7 → 7
  • m7 → dom7: raise the 3rd a half-step (♭3 → 3). The 7th doesn't move.
  • dom7 → maj7: raise the 7th a half-step (♭7 → 7). The 3rd doesn't move.

This is exactly the "♭7 vs natural 7" and "♭3 vs natural 3" confusion the lesson warns about — but as one moving note, not four chord shapes. Park on a root and toggle just the one note until each pair is reflexive.

5. Deriving any other key (don't memorize 36 boxes)

You never need the whole grid in your head. Carry one thing per chord and derive the rest:

  1. Find the 3rd by quality (♭3 for minor, natural 3 otherwise).
  2. The 7th is a perfect 5th away — unless it's a dom7, where it's a tritone.
  3. Cross-check with the ladder: if you know the dom7 pair, lower the 3rd for m7, or raise the 7th for maj7.

Any key you don't practice daily falls out of these three moves in seconds.

How to drill this (5 minutes)

  1. Name a random chord out loud ("A♭m7").
  2. Say just its 3rd and 7th before you touch the guitar (C♭ and G♭).
  3. Play the pair — notice whether it's tense (tritone → dominant) or open (perfect 5th → minor/major).
  4. Toggle one note to hear the neighboring quality (A♭m7 → A♭7 → A♭maj7).

When the pair arrives faster than you can second-guess it, you've got the Week 2 reflex — and you're ready to connect them between chords in Week 6.
