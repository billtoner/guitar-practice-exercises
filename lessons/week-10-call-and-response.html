The Recall Gap · Phase 3 · Week 10

Call & Response

Sing it, then find it — close the gap between ear and hands.

Phase 3 · Freedom & your voice. Part of the The Recall Gap 12-week plan.

This week's one goal: sing a phrase, then find it on the guitar — and trade 4-bar phrases with a recording (it calls, you respond; then you call and answer yourself).

Why this: your ear is already ahead of your hands. You can hum a line you can't yet play. Improvisation lives in closing that gap — playing what you hear instead of what your fingers happen to fall into. This is the week your solos stop being finger patterns and start being ideas.

Audiation — hear it first

Audiation is hearing music in your mind before you play it. Every strong improviser does it — the line exists as intention a beat before it exists as sound. The bridge from intention to instrument is sing-then-find: if you can sing it, you can learn to find it, and with reps the finding becomes near-instant.

Call-and-response is the training drill. A phrase is offered — by a recording, or by your own voice — and you answer it: same length, related idea. It forces real-time listening and responding, which is exactly what soloing over changes is.

Honest note: this week is mostly off the app

Fretsy has no microphone or sing-back feature — that's a deliberate design choice, not a gap to wait on. So the core of Week 10 is your voice + your guitar + a backing track + a recorder. Fretsy still helps at the edges: the Interval-by-Ear and Which-Degree-by-Ear drills sharpen the ear this week leans on. Use them as a warm-up, then put the app down and sing.

How to practice it

  • Sing a short, simple phrase (2–4 notes at first), then find it on the guitar. Repeat until finding it is fast.
  • Over a slow vamp: sing a 2-bar idea, then play it back.
  • Call-and-response with a track: let 4 bars play (the "call"), respond with your own 4 bars.
  • Advanced (Benson's signature): sing and play the same line at once — even roughly. Nothing locks ear to hands faster.

The daily session (30–45 min)

① Ear warm-up — 5 min · Drills tab

  • Interval-by-Ear / Which-Degree-by-Ear. Then put Fretsy down — the rest is voice and guitar.

② Sing-then-find — 10 min

  • Voice → guitar. Slow. No metronome needed early. Get the round-trip fast.

③ Over a track — 10–15 min

  • Sing an idea, then play it back. Then trade 4s with the recording. Record the session.

④ Reflect — 5 min

  • Listen back. Did what you played match what you sang? Where did the hands substitute a familiar pattern for the idea you actually heard?

Approach

Sing constantly, even out of tune — pitch accuracy improves quickly and isn't the point yet. The point is intention before execution. Keep tempos slow enough that you can genuinely hear a beat ahead.

Troubleshooting

  • "I can't sing in tune." Doesn't matter here — you're training the connection, not auditioning. It sharpens with reps.
  • "My hands play something different from what I sang." That gap, made audible, is the work. Slow down; sing shorter phrases; find them one note at a time.
  • "I run out of ideas responding to the call." Start by echoing the call almost exactly, then change one thing. A response isn't a brand-new invention — it's a reply.

End-of-week checkpoint ✅

  • Sing a short melody and find it on the guitar within a couple of tries.
  • Trade coherent 4-bar phrases with a backing track — your responses relate to the calls.
  • Caught yourself, at least once, playing a line you clearly heard first.

Not there yet? Shorten the phrases and slow the tempo. This one rewards patience more than any other week.
