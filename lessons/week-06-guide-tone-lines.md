# Week 6 — Guide-Tone Lines Through Changes

> **Phase 2 · Connecting & phrasing.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan.

**This week's one goal:** play a smooth **3rd-and-7th line** through a ii–V–I — moving from each chord's guide tone to the next chord's guide tone **by the nearest note**, with no big leaps.

**Why this is the smooth-jazz "glue":** last week you hit good notes *on* the downbeats. This week you **connect** them. When the 7th of one chord slides a half-step into the 3rd of the next, the harmony seems to move itself — that inevitable, gliding quality under Chuck Loeb and Peter White lines is mostly this. It's also the most economical thing on the guitar: the notes barely move.

---

## The concept — 7ths fall into 3rds

Over a ii–V–I, the guide tones are almost already a melody; you just have to *notice* it. In **Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7**:

| Chord | 3rd | 7th |
|---|---|---|
| Dm7 | F | C |
| G7 | B | F |
| Cmaj7 | E | B |

Two smooth lines fall out, each moving by a half or whole step:

```
Line A (7→3→…):  C  →  B  →  B      (7th of Dm7 → 3rd of G7 → 7th of Cmaj7)
Line B (3→7→…):  F  →  F  →  E      (3rd of Dm7 → 7th of G7 → 3rd of Cmaj7)
```

Notice the resolutions: the **7th of one chord drops a half-step to the 3rd of the next** (C→B, F→E). That falling half-step is the sound of a ii–V resolving. Nearest note wins — never jump when a neighbor will do.

> **Honest note on Fretsy:** the *line* is played over a moving progression, so the **Apply** block again uses an external ii–V–I backing track (YouTube / iReal Pro). Fretsy is perfect for the **reflex** half: drilling the 3rd and 7th of each chord (your Week 2 skill) in the progression's key so you can find the guide tones instantly.

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · **Drills tab**
- **Scale Degree** drill focused on **3rds and 7ths**. Beat the Clock ~7 s.
- Spoken: call the day's ii–V–I and recite both guide-tone lines (C→B→B, F→F→E) before playing.

### ② Reflex drill — 10 min · **Practice tab** *(guide tones, per chord)*
- Filter to the three 7th arpeggios; **Lock Key** to the day's key; Display **Degrees**.
- For each chord in the progression, find **only its 3rd and 7th** near your current hand position — don't run the whole arpeggio. Speed target: guide tones located in **under a second**, on both the 6th- and 5th-string-root shapes.

### ③ Apply — 10–15 min · **external ii–V–I backing track** *(build the line)*
- Play **only the guide-tone line** through the progression — two or three notes per cycle, all by nearest motion. It should feel almost too simple.
- When smooth, add **one passing or approach note** between guide tones (a single connector, not a scale run).
- Keep it slow (60–75 BPM). **Record 30 s;** listen for whether the line *glides* or *jumps*.

### ④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min
- **Interval by Ear** (Drills), focus on the half-step and whole-step — the intervals guide-tone lines live on.
- **Steal:** in a Peter White or Norman Brown phrase over a ii–V, find the falling half-step (7→3). Play it. That one resolution is a whole vocabulary.

---

## Tempo ladder (block ③)

```
Start:   60 BPM · bare guide-tone line, nearest note only
Step 2:  70 BPM · one passing note between guide tones
Step 3:  keep tempo, add a second key
Law:     if it leaps, you missed a nearer note — find it
```

---

## Troubleshooting

- **"My line jumps around."** You're grabbing the guide tone in a *far* octave. Stay in one small zone of the neck and take the **closest** 3rd/7th, even if it's on a different string right next to you.
- **"It sounds like an exercise, not music."** That's expected for a few days — the bare line is scaffolding. The single passing note (Day 5) and next week's approach notes turn it into a phrase.
- **"I lose which line I'm on."** Pick ONE line (A or B) and stay on it for the whole loop. Switching mid-stream is a Day-6 skill.
- **"I can't find the nearest note fast enough."** Back to Fretsy block ②: this is a guide-tone recall gap, and it's exactly what Week 2 built. Re-drill it.

## End-of-week checkpoint ✅

You're ready for Week 7 when you can:

- [ ] Play a **smooth guide-tone line** through a ii–V–I in two keys with **no leaps** — all half/whole-step motion.
- [ ] Hear and play the **7→3 falling resolution** on the ii–V.
- [ ] Insert **one passing note** between guide tones without losing the line.
- [ ] Use the guide-tone line as the **spine** of a short improvised phrase.

Your lines connect smoothly. Now we make them sound *intentional* — approaching each target chord tone from a fret above or below (and sometimes both, an "enclosure"). This is the bebop flavor threaded through Benson and Loeb, and it's what turns a correct line into a hip one.
