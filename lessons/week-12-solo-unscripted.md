# Week 12 — Solo Over a Real Song, Unscripted

> **Phase 3 · Freedom & your voice.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan.

**This week's one goal:** improvise a **full chorus** over a smooth-jazz tune you already play — **cold, no memorized solo** — record it, and compare it to your Week 1 clip.

**Why this:** this is the proof, and the integration. Everything from the last 11 weeks — instant chord tones, guide-tone lines, chromatic approaches, motif development, owned vocabulary, and space & tone — meets a real song form under real-time pressure. This week you find out that the recall gap has closed.

---

## The integration

You're no longer drilling ingredients; you're cooking. Over a tune's changes you'll target chord tones on strong beats (Wk 5–7), voice-lead guide tones through the ii–V's (Wk 6), state and develop a motif (Wk 8), reach for owned vocabulary when you want it (Wk 9), play lines you actually *hear* (Wk 10), and leave space with a warm tone (Wk 11).

You won't do all of it every chorus — nobody does. You'll do *some* of it, on purpose, and it will sound like music.

## The process

- **Pick the tune** — one you already play and love: a Benson, Peter White, Loeb, or Euge Groove number, or a standard you know cold.
- **Get a backing track** — solo over the real recording, an iReal Pro chart, or a YouTube backing track. *(This is exactly where your partner's future backing tracks — or Fretsy's planned "Changes Mode" as a visual guide — will eventually live.)*
- **Improvise a full chorus, cold.** No pre-planned solo. Record it.
- **Self-assess** against the rubric below. Then do it again.

## Fretsy's role

Minimal, by design — this is you, a tune, a track, and a recorder. Fretsy earned its keep in Weeks 1–9 building the reflexes you're now spending. Use its ear drills as a warm-up if you like, then play the music.

| Day | Focus |

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Ear/hands warm-up — 5 min
- A few arpeggio or ear reps to get loose. Nothing heavy.

### ② Know the form — 5–10 min
- Play through the tune's changes until the map is under your fingers. You can't improvise freely on a form you're still reading.

### ③ Improvise & record — 15 min · **over the track**
- A full chorus, cold, no script. Multiple takes. Let the bad ones be bad.

### ④ Self-assess — 5 min · **the rubric**
Rate each take honestly:

```
□ Did I land chord tones on strong beats?
□ Did a motif appear and develop?
□ Did it breathe — real space?
□ Warm, intentional tone?
□ Did it sound like ME?
```

---

## Approach

Don't chase perfect — chase *musical and honest*. A simple chorus that hits chord tones, breathes, and says something beats a flurry that says nothing. And compare only to your own **Week 1 recording**, never to Benson.

## Troubleshooting

- **"I get lost in the form."** Back to block ②. Freedom needs the map to be automatic. Simplify: outline just the guide tones through the changes until the form is second nature.
- **"It falls apart when I stop thinking."** Constrain the next take to one rule (e.g. chord tones on beat 1 only). Freedom still grows out of constraints.
- **"It doesn't sound like Benson."** It's not supposed to. It's supposed to sound like *you, now* — and better than you 12 weeks ago. That's the only fair comparison.

## End-of-week checkpoint ✅

- [ ] A recorded, improvised **full chorus** over a real tune — no memorized solo.
- [ ] It sounds intentional and musical: chord tones on strong beats, some space, a warm tone.
- [ ] **Audible growth** vs your Week 1 recording.
- [ ] You used, unplanned, at least **two** things from Weeks 5–11.

---

## Beyond Week 12: the loop keeps going

Improvisation isn't a finish line — it's a practice you return to. To keep growing:

- **Re-run the plan** at faster tempos and in new or harder keys; the reflexes deepen with each pass.
- **Learn and transcribe new tunes** — every tune teaches new vocabulary and new changes.
- **Build a repertoire** of songs you can solo over cold, and keep stealing licks from the players you love.
- **Keep recording.** Your monthly clips are the truest map of your progress — far more honest than how any single session felt.

You've closed the recall gap. Now you get to spend the rest of your playing life enjoying the freedom on the other side of it. 🎸
