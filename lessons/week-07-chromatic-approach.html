The Recall Gap · Phase 2 · Week 7

Chromatic Approach Notes

Approach targets from above and below — the bebop flavor in Benson & Loeb.

Phase 2 · Connecting & phrasing. Part of the The Recall Gap 12-week plan.

This week's one goal: approach your target chord tones from a fret above or below — landing the target on the beat, the approach note just before it. Then learn the enclosure: surround a target from both sides.

Why this is the "hip" ingredient: a correct note played bare sounds like an exercise; the same note approached sounds like Benson. Chromatic approaches are the bebop DNA running through George Benson and Chuck Loeb — a tiny lead-in that makes the target feel chosen and inevitable. You already know where the targets are (Weeks 5–6); this week is purely about the half-step run-up.

The concept — off-beat approach, on-beat target

The rule that makes it swing instead of stumble: the target lands ON the beat, the approach note lands OFF it (the "&" just before). Your ear forgives almost any approach note as long as it resolves to a chord tone on the beat.

Three flavors, easiest to spiciest:

Target = the 3rd of Cmaj7 (E), landing on beat 1

Below (chromatic):   D#  →  E       (a fret below, off-beat → on-beat)
Above (chromatic):   F   →  E       (a fret above)
Enclosure (both):    F  D#  →  E     (above, below, then target)

The enclosure (from above, then below, then the target) is the signature move — it "brackets" the note so the arrival feels earned. Keep approaches to one or two notes; this is seasoning, not a scale run.

Honest note on Fretsy: the targets live in a moving progression, so Apply stays on an external vamp/ii–V–I backing track. But Fretsy's metronome + arpeggio content is ideal for the mechanical drill: it keeps your targets honest (real chord tones) and the click keeps the on-beat/off-beat placement strict. The daily session (30–45 min)

① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · Drills tab

  • Spot the Wrong Note or Scale Degree to keep chord-tone vs non-chord-tone vision sharp — you need to know which note is the target and which is the approach.
  • Spoken: pick a target (e.g. "3rd of G7 = B"), name the fret above and below it.

② Reflex drill — 10 min · Practice tab + metronome (the mechanical move)

  • Filter to 7th arpeggios, Lock Key, Display Degrees, metronome on.
  • Pick one chord tone as the target. Play approach-note (on the "&") → target (on the beat), over and over, so the placement is automatic. Do it below, then above, then as an enclosure.
  • The click is the referee: target must land exactly on the beat every time.

③ Apply — 10–15 min · external backing track (make it music)

  • Over the day's vamp/ii–V–I, target chord tones on the downbeats (Week 5) and precede each with an approach (or an enclosure from Day 4 on).
  • Don't approach everything — one good approached target per bar beats a nonstop chromatic scramble. Record 30 s; check that targets still land on the beat.

④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min

  • Interval by Ear (Drills): drill the half-step, the whole engine of chromatic approaches.
  • Steal: find one enclosure in a Benson line (he does them constantly). Learn those 3 notes and move them to two other keys.

Tempo ladder (blocks ② and ③)

Start:   60 BPM · single approach → target, on the beat
Step 2:  70 BPM · enclosure (2 approach notes) → target
Step 3:  80 BPM · approaches inside a ii–V, sparingly
Law:     target ON the beat, approach OFF it — always

Troubleshooting

  • "It sounds sloppy / random." Your target isn't landing on the beat — the approach is stealing the downbeat. Slow down and let the click prove the target is on 1.
  • "I'm playing chromatic runs, not approaches." Cap it at one or two approach notes. If you can't hum where you're going, you've turned seasoning into soup.
  • "The enclosure trips me up." Practice it out of time first — above, below, target — then add the click. Order matters more than speed.
  • "It sounds outside/wrong." An approach note is fine only because it resolves. If it doesn't reach a chord tone on the beat, it just sounds like a mistake. Fix the landing, not the approach.

End-of-week checkpoint ✅

You're ready for Week 8 when you can:

  • Approach a target chord tone from above and from below, landing the target cleanly on the beat.
  • Play an enclosure (above + below + target) in time at a moderate tempo.
  • Use approaches into guide tones through a ii–V without losing the beat.
  • Do it sparingly — as seasoning, not a constant chromatic run.
