Arpeggios Across the Neck

One arpeggio, many doorways — coverage in all 12 keys.

Phase 1 · Foundations of automaticity. Part of the The Recall Gap 12-week plan. Previous: Week 2 — Guide Tones.

This week's one goal: connect the 6th- and 5th-string shapes (and beyond) into one arpeggio that spans the fretboard — so you can play any m7/dom7/maj7 in at least two positions, in all 12 keys.

Why this: improvisation needs neck coverage. If you can only play a Gmaj7 arpeggio in one box, then when a phrase or a chord change leaves you somewhere else on the neck, you freeze. Great players are never trapped — wherever their hand happens to be, the chord tones are right there. This week removes the "wrong part of the neck" excuse for good.

The concept — one arpeggio, many doorways

You already own two doorways into each arpeggio: the 6th-string root and the 5th-string root. They aren't separate things to memorize — they're the same notes in different places, and they overlap. The skill this week is linking: playing up through the 6th-string shape and, instead of stopping, shifting into the 5th-string shape (and onward) so the arpeggio keeps climbing the neck as one continuous line.

  • Think of it as CAGED-style position linking — each shape hands off to the next where they share notes.
  • The shift point is usually a shared chord tone: you land on a note that belongs to both shapes and let your hand slide into the new position there.
  • Your anchors are still the guide tones from Week 2. As you move across the neck, keep asking "where's the nearest 3rd? the nearest 7th?" — that's what keeps you oriented instead of just running patterns.

Two positions is the goal this week; more is a bonus. Depth (two rock-solid positions in all keys) beats breadth (five shaky ones).

The daily session (30–45 min)

① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · Drills tab

  • Name the Note across the whole neck (not just low positions) — you need to know where every note lives to link shapes.
  • Timer: Beat the Clock, 10 s → 5 s.
  • Micro-drill: pick a random key, name where the root sits on both the 6th and 5th strings before you play.

② Reflex drill — 10 min · Practice tab (the core of the week)

  • Lock Key (days 1–3) or unlock Key (days 4–7); Zoom whole neck; step CAGED positions.
  • Play the arpeggio in position 1, then link into position 2 without stopping — find the shared note and shift there.
  • Direction Both. Keep the guide tones (3 & 7) in mind as your anchors in each position.
  • Recall test: Hands-free auto-advance, ~4 s → 2 s — but now the win is landing the arpeggio in whichever position is nearest your hand, not always the low one.

③ Apply — 10–15 min · Practice tab (cover the whole neck)

  • Loop ON, Pattern In 3rds / Groups of 4, Direction Both, Zoom whole neck.
  • Run the arpeggio from the lowest position to the highest and back as one flowing line. Then let the key randomize and do it cold.
  • Improv seed: over an external m7 vamp, deliberately start your phrases in a high position one time and a low position the next — prove to yourself you're not trapped.

④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min · Drills tab

  • Chord Quality by Ear as usual (keep the ear sharp).
  • Steal: take a Norman Brown or George Benson lick and find it in two different places on the neck. Same notes, different doorway.

Tempo ladder (blocks ② and ③)

Start:   60 BPM · one position, then link to the next cleanly
Rule:    clean link 3× in a row → Speed Trainer +5 BPM
Ceiling: ~90 BPM across two positions, then add a third position
Law:     a clean shift beats a fast fumble — the join is the skill

Troubleshooting

  • "I lose my place when I shift positions." Slow the shift way down and find the shared note that lives in both shapes — that note is your handoff. Practice just the two-note hand-off in isolation.
  • "Some keys feel impossible." Those are exactly the keys worth the most reps. Lock the hard key for a whole session; familiarity is the only cure.
  • "The high positions feel cramped." They are — the frets are closer together. Adjust your hand, don't avoid them. Benson lives up there.
  • "I default to the same box every time." Force the issue: forbid yourself the low position for one Apply block and only play above the 7th fret.

End-of-week checkpoint ✅

You're ready for Week 4 when you can:

  • Play any m7 / dom7 / maj7 arpeggio in at least two positions, in any of the 12 keys.
  • Link two positions into one continuous line, ascending and descending, cleanly.
  • Do it at ~90 BPM without the shift falling apart.
  • Start an improvised phrase from a high or low position on demand.

Not all four yet? Stay — neck coverage is what turns "I know the arpeggio" into "I can use it anywhere."
