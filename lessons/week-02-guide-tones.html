Guide Tones

The 3rd & 7th — the two notes that carry the harmony.

Phase 1 · Foundations of automaticity. Part of the The Recall Gap 12-week plan. Previous: Week 1 — Seventh-Chord Arpeggios.

This week's one goal: for any m7, dom7, or maj7 you name, instantly find just its 3rd and 7th — drop the root and 5th entirely — on the 6th- and 5th-string root sets, in under a second.

Why this: the 3rd and 7th are the two notes that define a chord (they're what your ear hears as "minor" vs "dominant" vs "major"), and they're the smooth-jazz voice-leading targets — the notes Benson, White, and Loeb lean on to outline changes with almost nothing else. Stripping four notes down to two removes half the choices, which is exactly how recall gets faster. And it's the direct setup for Week 6, where you'll connect these two notes between chords.

The concept — two notes do the work

Last week you learned the full arpeggio (1–3–5–7). This week we throw away the root and the 5th, because they're the least informative notes — the 5th is the same in all three qualities, and the root is just the name. What's left is the sound:

ChordGuide tones (3rd · 7th)
m7♭3 · ♭7
dom73 (natural) · ♭7
maj73 (natural) · 7 (natural)

Two more things to notice, because they're the seed of everything later:

  • The 3rd and 7th of a chord sit a predictable distance apart — once you find one, the other has a reliable relationship to it. Learn to grab them as a pair, not two separate searches.
  • Between two chords a step apart, the guide tones barely move — often a half or whole step. That tiny motion is voice leading, and it's why a great player can imply a whole progression with two notes. You'll formalize this in Week 6; this week, just start noticing it.

The daily session (30–45 min)

① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · Drills tab

  • Scale Degree drill — answer "which note is the 3rd / the 7th" as fast as you can. The naming half of guide-tone recall.
  • Timer: Beat the Clock — start 10 s, tighten toward 5 s.
  • Spoken micro-drill: say a chord ("A♭m7"), then say just its 3rd and 7th out loud (C♭/B and G♭) before you touch the guitar.

② Reflex drill — 10 min · Practice tab (the core of the week)

  • Lock Key to today's key; Display Degrees; Neck ON; Zoom single position.
  • Draw an arpeggio, then play only its 3rd and 7th — root and 5th stay silent. Find the pair, play it, move on.
  • Alternate the 6th- and 5th-string root sets (5 min each).
  • Recall test: Hands-free auto-advance, countdown ~4 s → 2 s. The win: land both guide tones before the count-in ends.

③ Apply — 10–15 min · Practice tab (guide tones as music)

  • Over an external m7 vamp in today's key, improvise using only the 3rd and 7th (plus their neighbors). Two notes, all the phrasing you can find — space, rhythm, bends.
  • You'll be shocked how much it already sounds like a real line. That's the point: harmony lives in the guide tones. (Fretsy's planned "Changes Mode" will make this an in-app drill.)

④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min · Drills tab

  • Chord Quality by Ear (m7 / dom7 / maj7) — but this week, consciously listen to the 3rd and 7th to make the call.
  • Steal: find a short Benson or Chuck Loeb phrase and mark which notes are the 3rd or 7th of the underlying chord. You'll find they land there constantly.

Tempo ladder (blocks ② and ③)

Start:   60 BPM · find the pair on beat 1, hold it
Rule:    clean & instant 3× in a row → Speed Trainer +5 BPM
Ceiling: ~100 BPM, then move to the other string set / next quality
Law:     recall speed is the goal — never trade it for volume of notes

Troubleshooting

  • "I can find the 3rd but the 7th is slow." Learn them as a pair off the root, not two separate hunts — the 7th sits a predictable distance from the 3rd for each quality. Drill the pair, not the notes.
  • "♭7 vs natural 7 keeps tripping me." That one step is the whole difference between dom7 and maj7. Park on one root and toggle just that note until the two locations are reflexive.
  • "Two notes feels too limiting to 'improvise' with." That limit is the training — it forces phrasing and space instead of running scales. Peter White builds whole passages this way.
  • "Fretsy shows all four notes and it's distracting." Use Degrees display and consciously look only at the 3 and 7; treat the root/5th dots as scenery.

End-of-week checkpoint ✅

You're ready for Week 3 when you can:

  • Name any m7 / dom7 / maj7 in all 12 keys and land its 3rd and 7th in under ~1 second.
  • Do it on more than one string set (6th- and 5th-string roots).
  • Improvise a short, musical phrase over an m7 vamp using only guide tones.
  • Hear the 3rd/7th clearly enough to name a chord's quality by ear most of the time.

Not all four yet? Stay here — guide-tone recall is the highest-leverage reflex in the whole plan.
