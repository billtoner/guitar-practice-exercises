# Week 5 — Chord-Tone Targeting

> **Phase 2 · Connecting & phrasing.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan.

**This week's one goal:** over a moving progression, **land a chord tone on beat 1 of every chord.** Nothing fancier. If the chord is Dm7 and you're on D, F, A, or C when the bar turns over — you win.

**Why this is the hinge of the whole plan:** Phase 1 gave you the vocabulary (arpeggios, guide tones) as reflexes. Targeting is the moment that vocabulary meets *time* — it's the actual act of improvising over changes. Every other Phase 2 and Phase 3 skill (guide-tone lines, chromatic approaches, motifs) is decoration hung on this one hook. Get this and you are, by any honest definition, improvising.

---

## The concept — strong beats carry the harmony

A listener hears the chord change on the **downbeat**. If your note there belongs to the chord, the line sounds "right" no matter what happens in between; if it doesn't, no amount of fast noodling rescues it. So we practice the opposite of what most players do: instead of running scales and hoping, you **aim** — pick a target chord tone for beat 1 *before the bar arrives*, and let everything else be filler for now.

Start by aiming for the **3rd or the 7th** on beat 1 (the guide tones from Phase 1) — they state the chord's quality most clearly. The root and 5th are safe but bland; the 3rd and 7th are the ones that sing.

> **Honest note on Fretsy:** this week needs a *moving* backing — a two-chord vamp, then a ii–V–I — and Fretsy doesn't yet play progressions. So the **Apply** block uses an external slow backing track (YouTube "Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 slow backing track," or iReal Pro). Fretsy still does the heavy lifting on the **reflex** side: re-drilling the arpeggios of each chord so your targets are honest. This is the exact hole Fretsy's planned **Changes Mode** is designed to fill.

## The week at a glance

Build up from one target to a full ii–V–I. Keep the backing **slow** (60–80 BPM) all week — targeting on the beat is the skill, not speed.

| Day | Backing | Your single rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-chord m7 vamp (e.g. Dm7) | A chord tone on beat 1 of every bar |
| 2 | Two-chord vamp (Dm7 ↔ Gm7) | A chord tone on beat 1 of *each* chord |
| 3 | Two-chord vamp | Make beat 1 the **3rd or 7th** |
| 4 | Major ii–V–I (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7) | Chord tone on beat 1 of all three |
| 5 | ii–V–I in a second key (e.g. Am7–D7–Gmaj7) | Chord tone on beat 1 |
| 6 | ii–V–I, aim for 3rds/7ths | Guide tone on beat 1 |
| 7 | ii–V–I, two keys | Beat-1 targets, no stopping |

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · **Drills tab**
- **Scale Degree** and **Name the Note**, Beat the Clock ~7 s. Keep the recall reflex warm.
- Spoken: name the day's progression and say each chord's **3rd and 7th** out loud before you touch the guitar.

### ② Reflex drill — 10 min · **Practice tab** *(honest targets)*
- Content filter to **Maj-7th / Dom-7th / Min-7th arpeggios**. **Lock Key** to the day's key.
- Play the arpeggio of **each chord in today's progression** in turn (e.g. Dm7, then G7, then Cmaj7), Display **Degrees**, so beat-1 candidates (R/3/5/7) are burned in. Metronome 70 BPM.
- Find each chord's **3rd and 7th** near where the previous chord left off — you're pre-loading the targeting muscle.

### ③ Apply — 10–15 min · **external backing track** *(the real work)*
- Loop today's backing (slow). Improvise with the **single rule** from the table — a chord tone on beat 1, *everything else can be silence.* Genuinely: play a target note, rest, wait for the next downbeat, target again.
- Once solid, allow simple filler between targets — but the beat-1 landing is sacred.
- **Record 30 seconds every day.** Listen back for one thing only: was beat 1 a chord tone?

### ④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min
- **Chord Quality by Ear** (Drills) to keep the m7/dom7/maj7 sound sharp.
- **Steal:** find a spot in a Benson or Chuck Loeb solo where the change happens and notice which chord tone they land on. Nine times out of ten it's a 3rd or 7th. Copy that one move.

## Tempo ladder (block ③)

```
Start:   60 BPM backing · target beat 1 only, rest between
Step 2:  70–80 BPM · add light filler between targets
Rule:    a target you hit clean at 60 beats a scramble at 100
Law:     the downbeat is the exam; the rest is scratch paper
```

## Troubleshooting

- **"I get lost and miss the change."** Simplify — go back to the one-chord vamp, or count the bars out loud. Missing the change means the backing is too fast or too long; shrink it.
- **"My beat-1 note is always the root."** Fine for two days, then ban the root: force yourself onto the **3rd or 7th**. That's where the music is.
- **"It sounds empty."** Good — empty and *correct* is the goal this week. Filler comes in Weeks 7–8. Resist decorating before the target is automatic.
- **"I can hear it's wrong but can't fix it in time."** You're improving — hearing the miss is step one. Slow the backing until your hand catches up to your ear.

## End-of-week checkpoint ✅

You're ready for Week 6 when you can:

- [ ] Over a slow **ii–V–I** in two keys, land a **chord tone on beat 1** of each chord most of the time, without stopping.
- [ ] Make that beat-1 note a **3rd or 7th** on demand, not just the root.
- [ ] Do it while leaving space — you don't need constant notes to stay oriented.
- [ ] Hear, on your daily recording, that the downbeats "agree" with the chords.

Not there yet? Slow the backing and stay another few days. Targeting is the skill the rest of the plan stands on — it's worth over-learning.
