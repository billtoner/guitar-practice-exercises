# Week 4 — Dorian & Mixolydian Around the Arpeggio

> **Phase 1 · Foundations of automaticity.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan. Previous: [Week 3 — Arpeggios Across the Neck](week-03-arpeggios-across-neck.md).

**This week's one goal:** wrap the **scale around the arpeggio** — **Dorian** over m7, **Mixolydian** over dom7 (and a touch of **Ionian/Lydian** over maj7) — while the chord tones stay the target you resolve to.

**Why this:** the arpeggio gives you the *right* notes, but jumping between them sounds like an exercise. The scale fills the gaps so your lines *flow* — the passing notes are the connective tissue between chord tones. But here's the discipline that separates smooth-jazz phrasing from noodling: the scale tones are how you travel; the **chord tones are where you land.** This is the last brick of Phase 1 and the doorway to targeting in Phase 2.

---

## The concept — skeleton and connective tissue

For each chord quality there's a "home" scale that contains the arpeggio and adds the notes between:

| Chord | Home scale | The scale = arpeggio + … |
|---|---|---|
| **m7** | **Dorian** | the 1–♭3–5–♭7 plus 2, 4, and the bright 6 |
| **dom7** | **Mixolydian** | the 1–3–5–♭7 plus 2, 4, 6 |
| **maj7** | **Ionian** (or **Lydian** for a lift) | the 1–3–5–7 plus 2, 4(♯4 in Lydian), 6 |

Mental model:

- The **arpeggio is the skeleton** — the notes that are always "safe" and define the chord.
- The **scale tones are passing notes** — great in motion, but they want to *resolve* to a chord tone.
- **Land chord tones on strong beats** (beat 1, beat 3). Let the scale tones fall on the weak beats and the "&"s. Do that and even a plain scale run starts to sound intentional.
- The two notes that most want resolving are the scale's "tension" tones (the 4th over a maj7, for instance) — leaning on them and then resolving is exactly the Benson/Loeb move.

You already play these scales. The new skill is *hearing them as decoration around the arpeggio* rather than as patterns in their own right.

## How to use Fretsy

Add the two modes to your practice pool, but keep drilling them **in the same key and position as the matching arpeggio** so the relationship stays visible.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Practice content | Add **Dorian** and **Mixolydian** alongside the 7th-arpeggios | Scale and its chord's arpeggio, side by side |
| Display | **Degrees** | See which scale notes are chord tones (1·3·5·7) vs passing tones |
| Drill | **Build the Scale / Mode** | Reinforces the scale's spelling and where the chord tones sit inside it |
| Zoom | **Single position** first, then whole neck | Learn the scale-around-arpeggio shape in one place, then spread it |
| Pattern | **Straight**, then **In 3rds** | 3rds naturally emphasize chord tones — a bridge to musical phrasing |

**Honest caveat:** Fretsy will happily play a scale as its own thing; the *connection* to the arpeggio is the mental work you bring. Draw the arpeggio, then draw its home scale in the same key/position, and consciously see the extra notes filling the gaps.

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Warm-up · Fretboard flash — 5 min · **Drills tab**
- **Build the Scale / Mode** — spell Dorian and Mixolydian and, as you go, call out which notes are chord tones.
- Timer: **Beat the Clock**, **10 s → 5 s**.
- Micro-drill: name a chord, then name its home scale *and* the one or two scale notes that most want to resolve.

### ② Reflex drill — 10 min · **Practice tab** *(the core of the week)*
- **Lock Key**; draw the **arpeggio**, play it; then draw the **matching mode** in the same key/position and play it, *seeing the extra notes as fill*.
- Display **Degrees**, Zoom **single position** (spread to whole neck later in the week).
- **Recall test:** Hands-free auto-advance — name the chord, then play its scale but **stop on a chord tone** each time the count-in lands.

### ③ Apply — 10–15 min · **Practice tab** *(scale in motion, chord tones on the beat)*
- Over an external **m7 vamp** (then a **dom7** vamp), improvise using the full **Dorian** (then **Mixolydian**) — but make **beat 1 of each bar a chord tone**, ideally the 3rd or 7th.
- Use rests. A scale run that pauses on a guide tone sounds like music; one that never lands sounds like practice. *(Fretsy's planned "Changes Mode" will let you drill this against moving chords in-app.)*

### ④ Ear + steal — 5–10 min · **Drills tab**
- **Scale/Mode by Ear** — start telling Dorian from Mixolydian (listen for the natural 3rd vs ♭3 and the ♭7 they share).
- Steal: grab a flowing Chuck Loeb or Peter White run and mark where the scale tones *pass through* and where the line *lands* on a chord tone.

## Tempo ladder (blocks ② and ③)

```
Start:   60 BPM · scale up, chord tone on beat 1
Rule:    lands are clean & intentional 3× in a row → Speed Trainer +5 BPM
Ceiling: ~90 BPM, then add In-3rds phrasing at 60 and climb again
Law:     scale tones travel, chord tones land — never blur the two
```

## Troubleshooting

- **"It just sounds like scales again."** You're not resolving. Force a chord tone onto every beat 1; the scale instantly stops sounding like an exercise.
- **"Which scale over which chord?"** Dorian→m7, Mixolydian→dom7, Ionian/Lydian→maj7. When in doubt, fall back to the arpeggio — the chord tones are always right.
- **"Dorian and Mixolydian feel the same."** They share the ♭7; the giveaway is the **3rd** (♭3 in Dorian, natural in Mixolydian). Toggle just that note to feel the difference.
- **"I keep landing on tension notes."** Good ear-training moment — notice the itch, then resolve it down or up a step to a chord tone. That resolution *is* the smooth-jazz sound.
