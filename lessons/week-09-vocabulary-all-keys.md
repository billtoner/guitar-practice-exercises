# Week 9 — Vocabulary in All Keys

> **Phase 3 · Freedom & your voice.** Part of [The Recall Gap](the-recall-gap-plan.md) 12-week plan.

**This week's one goal:** take the **5 best licks** you've banked from the daily "steal," and make each one *yours* — playable in **all 12 keys** and in **2 positions**, and available on demand mid-solo.

**Why this:** the pros don't run scales — they speak in **phrases**. Benson, Chuck Loeb, Peter White all draw from a personal library of licks they own so completely that the right one arrives without thought. Scales give you *notes*; vocabulary gives you *sentences*. This is the week you stop playing exercises and start playing language.

---

## Vocabulary transposes by meaning, not by shape

A lick memorized as "frets 5–7–8 on the G string" is trapped in one key. The same lick understood as "♭7 → root → 3rd over the m7" moves anywhere, because you're moving the *meaning*, not the fingering. Relate every note of each lick to the chord tone or scale degree it lands on. Once you know a lick as a sequence of degrees, transposing it is just starting from a new root.

Move each lick around the **circle of 4ths** (C → F → B♭ → E♭ → A♭ …) — the same motion jazz harmony travels, so it doubles as ear training.

## How to practice it

- Pick your 5 strongest banked licks — short, 3 to 7 notes each.
- For each, **label its degrees** against the chord it sits over (e.g. over Dm7, the notes might be ♭7–1–♭3–5). Say the degrees out loud as you play.
- Take **one** lick through all 12 keys, slowly, in position 1 — then again in position 2.
- New lick each day; by day 5 you've cycled all five. Days 6–7 combine and apply.

## The daily session (30–45 min)

### ① Warm-up · Degree recall — 5 min · **Drills tab**
- **Scale Degree** (or Chord-tone) drill — keep the degree-naming reflex sharp. Labeling licks by degree is the whole method this week.

### ② Transpose — 15 min · **guitar + Fretsy**
- Today's lick through all 12 keys in **circle-of-4ths** order, saying degrees aloud.
- Use Fretsy's **Degrees display** with a locked key to *confirm* the lick's chord tones land where you think they do.

### ③ Second position — 10 min
- The same lick in a different area of the neck. Vocabulary you can only play in one spot is only half-owned.

### ④ Apply — 5–10 min · **over a vamp**
- Loop an m7 or ii–V vamp (external track) and drop today's lick in — first on purpose, then try to make it feel spontaneous. Record 30 seconds.

---

## Approach

Slow and correct. Transposing sloppily bakes in errors you'll fight later. If a key resists (usually the flat/sharp-heavy ones), that's the key worth an extra minute. Speed is irrelevant this week — **access** is the goal.

## Troubleshooting

- **"I can play the lick but freeze in odd keys."** You memorized shape, not meaning. Re-map its degrees and say them aloud.
- **"It sounds pasted-in when I use it."** Good ears. Vary it — change one note, the rhythm, or where it starts in the bar. A lick you can bend is owned; one you can only quote is borrowed.
- **"Five licks feels like a lot."** Then own **3 cold** rather than 5 loosely. Depth beats breadth.

## End-of-week checkpoint ✅

- [ ] Play all 5 licks fluently in all 12 keys, knowing the degrees.
- [ ] Each lick in at least **2 positions** on the neck.
- [ ] Used at least one lick **spontaneously** (not planned) in an improv over a track.

Not there yet? Trim to the licks you love most and own those completely.
